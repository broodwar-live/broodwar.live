## Overview

PvP is built around Dragoons, Reavers and early tech choices. Early mistakes — missing a Dark Templar, losing Probes to a Reaver, or falling behind on Dragoon range — are hard to recover from.

## Key Concepts

- **Dragoons** — the core unit; the range upgrade (Singularity Charge) is a priority.
- **Reavers** — a Scarab deals 100 damage (125 with the upgrade) with splash. That kills Probes in groups but does not one-shot a full-health Dragoon (100 HP, 80 shields). Liquipedia describes Snow, an ASL regular, as having the greatest Reaver micro of any Protoss.
- **Dark Templar** — permanently cloaked; without Observers or Cannons they can wipe out Probes.
- **Scouting** — the first Probe scout and later Observers decide how to respond to the opponent's tech.

## Common Openings

- **2 Gate Reaver** — a family of builds that train Dragoons with range plus a Shuttle and Reaver, using the Reaver to support the army rather than only to harass. A typical "Gate–Robo–Gate" order: Pylon 8, Gateway 10, Assimilator 11, Cybernetics Core 13, Dragoon range at 21, Robotics Facility at 25, second Gateway at 29.
- **Dark Templar rush** — fast Templar Archives for Dark Templar; punishes a player without detection.
- **Proxy Gateways / Zealot rushes** — cheese that wins quickly if unscouted.

## Mid Game

- **Dragoon fights** — positioning matters: the side with more space can bring more Dragoons into range at once, so luring the opponent through a choke is a big advantage.
- **Reaver drops** — Shuttles keep Reavers mobile; dropping, firing and picking the Reaver back up limits losses.
- **Observers** — needed both to scout and to detect Dark Templar.

## Late Game

PvP rarely goes long, but Storm, Archons and occasionally Arbiters appear in extended games. Storm is a strong answer to Shuttle/Reaver play because both units take full damage from it.

## Associated Players

- **IntoTheRainbow** — Liquipedia credits him with popularising the Reaver drop.
- **Snow** — noted for Reaver micro.
